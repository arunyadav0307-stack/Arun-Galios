# §1. Research Gap and Precise New Problem

## 1.1 The gap

The three source papers form a complete arc for **dimensions** and **averages**, but leave **enumeration** open — and they say so twice, in their own conclusions:

- **P1** solves enumeration only for $l=4$, and only by a 14-set inclusion–exclusion over 7 interval cases with separate even/odd-$q$ variants. It is not extensible: the number of cases grows with the divisor structure of $l$, and the authors explicitly leave "enumeration … over all finite fields" open.
- **P2** explicitly leaves the counting problem open over the affine algebra ring $A$.
- **P3** computes the *mean* but never the *distribution*, and its mean is taken over an artificially **restricted** sample space $\mathcal C(n,q,\lambda)$ (adjacent exponents forced distinct) introduced solely to make a borrowed expectation lemma applicable.

The deeper structural gap: **in P1/P2/P3 the hull dimension is always manipulated as a single global expression** $n-\deg\operatorname{lcm}(g,h^{\#})$. None of the three papers observes that this quantity *splits as a sum of independent, bounded, purely local contributions*, one per Galois-reciprocal cycle. Consequently none of them has access to generating functions, transfer matrices, or limit laws.

## 1.2 The precise new problem

> **Problem (Hull Enumerator Problem).** Let $q=p^e$, $0\le k<e$, $n=n'p^{\nu}$ with $\gcd(n',p)=1$, and $\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^{*}$ with $r=\operatorname{ord}(\lambda)$, $r\mid(1+p^{e-k})$, $\gcd(n',r)=1$. Let $\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}$ be the set of all $\lambda$-constacyclic codes of length $n$ over $\mathbb F_q$. Determine **exactly**
> $$N_{n,q,\lambda,k}(y)\;:=\;\sum_{C\in\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}}y^{\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)},$$
> i.e. the number of $\lambda$-constacyclic codes of each prescribed $k$-Galois hull dimension — uniformly in $l$ (not just $l=4$), and with the same question over the affine algebra ring $A$ (P2) and over $R_{m,q}$ (P3).
>
> **Derived sub-problems.** (i) Exact counts of $k$-Galois LCD, self-orthogonal, and dual-containing members of $\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}$; (ii) exact moments (mean, variance) and the limit law of $\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)$ for $C$ uniform on $\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}$; (iii) an algorithm computing $N$ in time polynomial in $\log|\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}|$, as opposed to enumerating $|\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}|=(p^{\nu}+1)^{\sum_{t,j}a_t\beta_t(j)}$ codes.

## 2.1 N1 — The local "min-form" and additive splitting (the key move)

**[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE]** Using $P-\max\{u_m,P-u_{m-1}\}=\min\{u_{m-1},P-u_m\}$, P1's $b_{tij}$ becomes
$$b_{tij}\;=\;w_{a_t}\big(u_{tij,\cdot}\big),\qquad \boxed{\,w_a(u)\;:=\;\sum_{m=0}^{a-1}\min\{u_{m-1},\,P-u_m\}\,}\quad(\text{indices mod }a),$$
with $P:=p^{\nu}$. Hence P1 Thm 3 is *exactly* the additive statement
$$\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)\;=\;\sum_{(t,j,i)}\operatorname{ord}_j(q)\cdot w_{a_t}\big(u_{tij,\cdot}\big).$$
Each summand depends **only** on the exponent word of **one** Galois-reciprocal cycle. The number of cycles is $B=\sum_{t,j}\beta_t(j)$; the words are chosen independently and uniformly from $[0,P]^{a_t}$.

*Why this is new:* P1's Thm 3 is stated with the "$\max$" form and a global $n-\deg\operatorname{lcm}$; P2 Thm 8 and P3 Thm 4.9 copy that form verbatim. The $\min$-form turns a global degree computation into a **sum of independent bounded local weights** — the precondition for everything below.

## 2.2 N2 — The enumerator factorisation

**[EXPECTED, strongly supported]** Because the words are independent, the hull-dimension distribution factors:
$$N_{n,q,\lambda,k}(y)=\prod_{t=1}^{s}\ \prod_{j\in D_t}\ \Big(W_{a_t}^{(P)}\big(y^{\operatorname{ord}_j(q)}\big)\Big)^{\beta_t(j)},\qquad W_a^{(P)}(z):=\sum_{u\in[0,P]^a}z^{\,w_a(u)} .$$
This is a *complete and uniform* answer to the Hull Enumerator Problem, valid for **every** $l$, and simultaneously over $A$ and $R_{m,q}$ by adding the idempotent index (§3.7). It replaces P1's entire 14-set/7-interval inclusion–exclusion (which covers only $l=4$) by a one-line product.

## 2.3 N3 — Transfer-matrix structure of $W_a^{(P)}$

**[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE]** $w_a$ is a nearest-neighbour cyclic interaction $w_a(u)=\sum_m\phi(u_{m-1},u_m)$ with $\phi(x,y)=\min\{x,P-y\}$. Therefore
$$W_a^{(P)}(z)=\operatorname{tr}\!\big(T_P(z)^{a}\big),\qquad T_P(z)=\big(z^{\min\{x,\,P-y\}}\big)_{0\le x,y\le P}\in\mathbb Z[z]^{(P+1)\times(P+1)} .$$
Consequences (all **[EXPECTED]** unless marked):
- Bivariate generating function in $a$:
$$\mathcal G_P(z,t):=\sum_{a\ge1}W_a^{(P)}(z)\,t^{a}=\operatorname{tr}\!\big(tT_P(z)(I-tT_P(z))^{-1}\big)=\frac{\text{poly}}{\det(I-tT_P(z))},\quad \deg_t\det(I-tT_P(z))=P+1 .$$
  **[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE]** $\deg_t=P+1$ checked for $P=1,\dots,5$; at $z=1$, $T_P(1)=J_{P+1}$ so $\det(I-tT_P(1))=1-(P+1)t$.
- $W_a^{(P)}$ satisfies an explicit linear recurrence in $a$ of order $P+1$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Z[z]$.
- $W_a^{(P)}(1)=(P+1)^{a}$ (total word count) — consistent.
- $\deg W_a^{(P)}=\lfloor aP/2\rfloor$ **[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE, $P\le3$, $a\le5$]** — matching P1's bound $0\le b_{tij}\le\lfloor a_tp^{\nu}/2\rfloor$ and showing it is tight.
- $W_a^{(P)}$ is **not** palindromic in general **[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE: palindromic for $(P,a)=(1,2),(1,4),(3,1)$, not for $(1,3),(1,5),(2,2),(3,2)$]** — so palindromicity must **not** be claimed; only the degree and the constant term are safe universal statements.

## 2.4 N4 — Closed form in the (dominant) semisimple case $P=1$

**[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE]** When $\gcd(n,q)=1$ we have $P=p^{\nu}=1$ and $T_1(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\z&1\end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalues $1\pm\sqrt z$, giving
$$\boxed{\;W_a^{(1)}(z)=(1+\sqrt z)^{a}+(1-\sqrt z)^{a}=2\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}\binom{a}{2i}z^{i}\;}$$
Verified for $a=1,\dots,6$: coefficient lists $[2],[2,2],[2,6],[2,12,2],[2,20,10],[2,30,30,2]$. Since P1 Cor 1, P2 Cor 3.4 and P3 Cor 4.10 are all stated for $\gcd(n,q)=1$, this closed form covers the regime the literature mostly lives in, and makes every count an explicit convolution of binomials.

## 2.5 N5 — Exact counts of the three extremal classes

**[VERIFIED-IN-PROBE; proof in §4.2]**
- **LCD.** $[z^0]W_a^{(P)}=2$ for every $a\ge1$, $P\ge1$; the only weight-0 words are $0^{a}$ and $P^{a}$. Hence
$$\#\{k\text{-Galois LCD } \lambda\text{-constacyclic codes of length }n\}=2^{B},\qquad B=\sum_{t=1}^{s}\sum_{j\in D_t}\beta_t(j).$$
  Verified on 13 instances (Annex A.3, A.7). This is a clean closed-form count that P1 could only obtain in the $l=4$ case through its inclusion–exclusion machinery, and that P2/P3 never obtain at all (they give only sufficient conditions: P2 Thm 9, P3 Thm 4.14).
  > **OVERLAP DISCLOSURE (mandatory).** In the specialisation Euclidean/Hermitian, $\lambda=\pm1$, $P=1$, cycles have length only $1$ or $2$ and $B=s+t$; then $2^{B}$ **is exactly the factor $2^{s+t}$ of Sangwisut–Jitman–Ling–Udomkavanich, *Finite Fields Appl.* 33 (2015) 232–257 (PA-1)**. T5 is therefore a *generalisation* of their count to $k$-Galois duality, arbitrary $\lambda$, arbitrary cycle length $a_t$, and $P>1$ — **not a first**. The specialisation must be exhibited and PA-1 credited. Verified: our run on their instance reproduces $2^{s+t}=2^{4}=16$ (Annex A.7).
- **Self-orthogonal / dual-containing.** $C\subseteq C^{\perp_k}\iff h^{\#}\mid g\iff u_{m-1}+u_m\ge P$ for all $m$; $C^{\perp_k}\subseteq C\iff g\mid h^{\#}\iff u_{m-1}+u_m\le P$ for all $m$. Both are *local* conditions, so the counts are $\prod_{\text{cycles}}\operatorname{tr}(S_{\ge}^{a})$ and $\prod_{\text{cycles}}\operatorname{tr}(S_{\le}^{a})$ with $S_{\ge}[x,y]=[x+y\ge P]$, $S_{\le}[x,y]=[x+y\le P]$.
- **Quantum consequence (free of any minimum-distance computation).** In P2 Thm 10 the entanglement is $c=nN-k-\dim_q\operatorname{hull}_k(C)$, so $c=0$ **iff** the code is dual-containing. N5 therefore yields the **exact number of entanglement-free** ($c=0$) quantum codes obtainable from $\lambda$-constacyclic codes over $A$ and over $R_{m,q}$ — a new result requiring no Magma and no minimum-distance search.

## 2.6 N6 — Moments, and a corrected/unrestricted average

**[EXPECTED] $\mathbb E$** and $\operatorname{Var}$ are $\sum_{t,j}\big(\operatorname{ord}_j(q)\big)^{m}\cdot$ (logarithmic derivatives of $W_{a_t}^{(P)}$ at $1$). Two distinct payoffs:
- The **unrestricted** mean (over *all* constacyclic codes) is obtained — which is the object P3's Table 1 is actually reporting (see §7.2 and the audit flag F2).
- P3's **restricted** sample space is recovered *exactly* by replacing $T_P$ with $\tilde T_P:=T_P\circ(J-I)$ (Hadamard product with the off-diagonal indicator) for $a\ge2$, keeping $T_P$ for $a=1$. This reproduces P3's Thm 3.3/4.23 **within the same framework**, without P3's Lemma 3.2 (see audit flag F2).

## 2.7 N7 — Limit law, and a qualitative contrast with Sendrier

**[EXPECTED, highest-risk item]** Each cycle contributes a bounded independent summand, so the hull dimension is a sum of a triangular array of independent bounded variables → Lindeberg–Feller CLT + concentration. Since P3 Cor 3.5 gives mean $\Theta(n)$ (indeed $\le n/4$ in the coprime case), this yields the conceptual statement:

> For a uniformly random $\lambda$-constacyclic code, $\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)$ is $\Theta(n)$ with $O(\sqrt{\cdot})$ fluctuations, **in sharp contrast** to a uniformly random $q$-ary linear code, for which (Sendrier, *SIAM J. Discrete Math.* **10** (1997) 282–293, cited as P1 [21] / P3 [28]) the hull dimension is bounded in probability with an asymptotically positive constant proportion at each fixed $\ell$.

This contrast is, to our knowledge, not drawn anywhere in P1/P2/P3 and is a genuine conceptual contribution.

## 2.8 Novelty statement (one paragraph, for the introduction) — *revised after audit*

*We show that the hull-dimension formula of Debnath–Prakash–Islam, rewritten in the equivalent "min" form, decomposes the $k$-Galois hull dimension of a $\lambda$-constacyclic code into a sum of independent bounded contributions indexed by the Galois-reciprocal cycles of $x^n-\lambda$. This converts every counting question about Galois hulls into the study of one two-parameter family of polynomials $W_a^{(P)}$, which we show are traces of powers of an explicit $(P+1)\times(P+1)$ transfer matrix. We obtain: a uniform enumerator for the hull-dimension distribution, valid for all $l$ (the problem left open in P1 beyond $l=4$) and over the affine algebra ring $A$ (the problem left open in P2); the closed form $W_a^{(1)}=(1+\sqrt z)^a+(1-\sqrt z)^a$; exact counts of Galois LCD, self-orthogonal and dual-containing constacyclic codes; exact moments recovering and correcting the average-dimension formulas of P3; and a central limit theorem exhibiting $\Theta(n)$ growth, contrasting with Sendrier's $O(1)$ law for unstructured linear codes. In the intersection of our scope with that of Sangwisut–Jitman–Ling–Udomkavanich (Euclidean/Hermitian hulls of cyclic and negacyclic codes), our enumerator reduces to theirs and our LCD count $2^{B}$ specialises to their factor $2^{s+t}$; our contribution there is the extension to $k$-Galois duality, arbitrary $\lambda$, cycle lengths greater than two, and repeated-root lengths.*

# §3. Mathematical Framework

## 3.1 Standing data (adopted verbatim from P1 §3.1 / P2 §2.4 / P3 §3)

$q=p^e$ ($p$ prime, $e\ge1$); $k\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}$; $n=n'p^{\nu}$, $\gcd(n',p)=1$, $\nu\ge0$; $\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^{*}$, $r=\operatorname{ord}(\lambda)$, $r\mid(1+p^{e-k})$, $\gcd(n',r)=1$; $P:=p^{\nu}$; $\sigma:=(\cdot)^p$ the Frobenius; for $f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_ix^i$ with $f_0\neq0$,
$$f^{\#}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_0^{-\,p^{e-k}}f_i^{\,p^{e-k}}x^{m-i}\qquad(\text{equivalently } f^{\#}=f_0^{-\sigma^{e-k}}x^{m}\,f^{\sigma^{e-k}}(1/x)).$$
$l'$ = least multiple of $\operatorname{lcm}(e,e-k)$ with $l:=l'/(e-k)$ even; $a_1=1<a_2<\dots<a_s$ the positive divisors of $l$; $A$, $B_i$, $D_t=A\cap B_{a_t}$, $\beta_t(j)=\varphi(j)/(a_t\varphi(r)\operatorname{ord}_j(q))$, $\Delta_t=\sum_{j\in D_t}\varphi(j)/(a_t\varphi(r))$, $\sum_t a_t\Delta_t=n'$.

**Indexing convention.** A **cycle** is a pair $\mathfrak c=((f_0,f_1,\dots,f_{a-1}),\,d)$ where $f_m$ is monic irreducible of degree $d$, $f_{m+1}$ is the monic normalisation of $f_m^{\#}$, and $a$ is minimal with $f_a$ monic-equal to $f_0$. Its **length** is $a$, its **degree** is $d=\operatorname{ord}_j(q)$ for the unique $j\in D_t$ containing it, and its **weight** is $\operatorname{ord}_j(q)$. Let $\mathfrak C(n,q,\lambda,k)$ be the multiset of cycles. Then $|\mathfrak C|=B=\sum_{t,j}\beta_t(j)$ and $\sum_{\mathfrak c}a(\mathfrak c)=\#\{\text{distinct monic irreducible factors of }x^n-\lambda\}=\sum_{j\in A}\varphi(j)/(\varphi(r)\operatorname{ord}_j(q))$.

## 3.2 New definitions

**Definition 1 (cycle word).** For a cycle $\mathfrak c$ of length $a$, the *exponent word* of $C$ at $\mathfrak c$ is $u^{(\mathfrak c)}=(u_0,\dots,u_{a-1})\in[0,P]^{a}$, where $u_m$ is the exponent of $f_m$ in $g(x)$.

**Definition 2 (local weight).** For $a\ge1$, $P\ge1$, $u\in[0,P]^{a}$ (indices modulo $a$):
$$w_a^{(P)}(u):=\sum_{m=0}^{a-1}\min\{u_{m-1},\,P-u_m\}.$$
$w_a^{(P)}(u)\in\big[0,\ \lfloor aP/2\rfloor\big]$.

**Definition 3 (cycle polynomial).** $W_a^{(P)}(z):=\sum_{u\in[0,P]^a}z^{w_a^{(P)}(u)}\in\mathbb Z[z]$; $c_{a,i}^{(P)}:=[z^i]W_a^{(P)}$.

**Definition 4 (hull enumerator).** $N_{n,q,\lambda,k}(y):=\sum_{C}y^{\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)}$.

**Definition 5 (restricted cycle polynomial).** For $a\ge2$, $\tilde W_a^{(P)}:=\operatorname{tr}(\tilde T_P^{a})$ with $\tilde T_P:=T_P\circ(J-I)$ (diagonal zeroed); $\tilde W_1^{(P)}:=W_1^{(P)}$. This encodes P3's sample space $\mathcal C(n,q,\lambda)$.

> For $C\in\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}$ with generator $g$ as in P1 (5),
> $$\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)=\sum_{\mathfrak c\in\mathfrak C}\ \operatorname{ord}_{j(\mathfrak c)}(q)\cdot w_{a(\mathfrak c)}^{(P)}\big(u^{(\mathfrak c)}\big).$$

> **T2 (Enumerator factorisation). [EXPECTED; verified in probe on 7 instances]**
> $$N_{n,q,\lambda,k}(y)=\prod_{\mathfrak c\in\mathfrak C}W_{a(\mathfrak c)}^{(P)}\big(y^{\operatorname{ord}_{j(\mathfrak c)}(q)}\big)

> *Corollaries:* $N(1)=|\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}|=(P+1)^{\sum_{\mathfrak c}a(\mathfrak c)}$; the attainable-dimension set $D_k(n,q,\lambda)$ is the support of $N$, recovering **P2 Cor 3.5**.

> **T3 (Transfer matrix). [VERIFIED-IN-PROBE for $P\le4$, $a\le5$]**
> $W_a^{(P)}(z)=\operatorname{tr}(T_P(z)^a)$ with $T_P(z)=\big(z^{\min\{x,P-y\}}\big)_{0\le x,y\le P}$; $\mathcal G_P(z,t)=\sum_{a\ge1}W_a^{(P)}t^a=\operatorname{tr}(tT_P(I-tT_P)^{-1})$ is rational with denominator $\det(I-tT_P(z))$ of $t$-degree $P+1$; $W_a^{(P)}$ obeys an order-$(P+1)$ linear recurrence in $a$ over $\mathbb Z[z]$; $\deg W_a^{(P)}=\lfloor aP/2\rfloor$.

> **T4 (Semisimple closed form). [VERIFIED-IN-PROBE, $a\le6$]** For $P=1$: $W_a^{(1)}(z)=(1+\sqrt z)^a+(1-\sqrt z)^a=2\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}\binom{a}{2i}z^i$.
> *Cor (explicit low-dimension counts when all $\operatorname{ord}_j(q)=1$, $P=1$):* $N(0)=2^{B}$; $N(1)=2^{B-1}\sum_{\mathfrak c}\binom{a(\mathfrak c)}{2}$; and generally $N(L)$ is the $L$-th elementary-convolution coefficient of the binomial factors.

> **T5 (LCD count). [VERIFIED-IN-PROBE; proof in §4.2]** $c_{a,0}^{(P)}=2$ with extremal words $0^a,P^a$; hence $\#\{C:\dim\operatorname{hull}_k(C)=0\}=2^{B}$.

> **T6 (Self-orthogonal / dual-containing counts). [EXPECTED]**
> $\#\{C\subseteq C^{\perp_k}\}=\prod_{\mathfrak c}\operatorname{tr}(S_{\ge}^{a(\mathfrak c)})$, $\#\{C^{\perp_k}\subseteq C\}=\prod_{\mathfrak c}\operatorname{tr}(S_{\le}^{a(\mathfrak c)})$, $S_{\ge}[x,y]=[x+y\ge P]$, $S_{\le}[x,y]=[x+y\le P]$. *Cor:* the number of $c=0$ EAQECCs obtainable via P2 Thm 10 from $\lambda$-constacyclic codes over $A$ equals $\prod_{S(\nu)}\prod_{\mathfrak c}\operatorname{tr}(S_{\le}^{a(\mathfrak c)})$.

> **T7 (Moments). [EXPECTED]** With $\mu_a^{(P)}:=\frac{(W_a^{(P)})'(1)}{(P+1)^a}$ and $v_a^{(P)}:=\frac{(W_a^{(P)})''(1)+(W_a^{(P)})'(1)}{(P+1)^a}-(\mu_a^{(P)})^2$,
> $\mathbb E[\dim\operatorname{hull}_k]=\sum_{t,j}\operatorname{ord}_j(q)\beta_t(j)\mu_{a_t}^{(P)}$, $\operatorname{Var}=\sum_{t,j}\operatorname{ord}_j(q)^2\beta_t(j)v_{a_t}^{(P)}$.
> *Cor (recovery of P3):* replacing $W$ by $\tilde W$ for $a\ge2$ reproduces P3 Thm 3.3 / Thm 4.23; the unrestricted version gives the mean over *all* constacyclic codes (the quantity P3's Table 1 actually reports — see audit flag F2).

> **T8 (CLT). [EXPECTED — CONDITIONAL, highest risk]** Along any sequence of instances with total variance $V\to\infty$ and $\max_{\mathfrak c}\operatorname{ord}_{j(\mathfrak c)}(q)^2\lfloor a(\mathfrak c)P/2\rfloor^{2}\big/V\to0$, $\big(\dim\operatorname{hull}_k-\mathbb E\big)/\sqrt V\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$.

> **T9 (Algorithm). [EXPECTED]** $N$ is computable in time polynomial in $\log|\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}|$ and in $n,\log q$ (factor $x^n-\lambda$; group into cycles; evaluate $\operatorname{tr}(T^a)$ by binary powering in $\mathbb Z[z]$; multiply the $B$ factors). Naïve enumeration costs $(P+1)^{\sum a(\mathfrak c)}$.

## 3.5 Structural reductions available for the theory of $W_a^{(P)}$

Two exact factorisations were found during probing and should be developed in the paper (both **[EXPECTED]**; the first is elementary, the second is a computed observation needing proof):

1. **Hadamard/rank-one form.** $z^{\min\{x,P-y\}}=\prod_{i=1}^{P}\big(1+(z-1)\,a_i(x)b_i(y)\big)$ with $a_i(x)=[x\ge i]$, $b_i(y)=[y\le P-i]$. So $T_P(z)$ is a Hadamard product of $P$ rank-one perturbations of the all-ones matrix $J$.
2. **LDL$^\mathsf T$ form.** With $M=(z^{\min\{x,y\}})_{x,y=0}^{P}$, $L[x,i]=[x\ge i]$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,z-1,z(z-1),\dots,z^{P-1}(z-1))$ and $R$ the reversal permutation: $M=LDL^{\mathsf T}$, $T_P=MR$, and $\operatorname{tr}(T_P^{a})=\operatorname{tr}\big((DN)^{a}\big)$ where $N=L^{\mathsf T}RL$ is the Hankel matrix $N[i,j]=(P+1-i-j)^{+}$. **[EXPECTED — needs verification.]**

Both are optional depth-enhancers; the paper does **not** depend on them.

## 3.6 The restricted / unrestricted dichotomy

P3's $\mathcal C(n,q,\lambda)$ is a **proper** subset of $\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}$ (it excludes words with cyclically adjacent equal exponents). The blueprint computes **both** throughout and reports them separately. This is not a cosmetic distinction: the two means genuinely differ (e.g. for $P=3$: unrestricted $\mathbb E[\max\{u,P-v\}]=34/16$, restricted $=24/12$; see audit flag F2).

# §4. Feasible Proof / Derivation Strategy

## 4.1 Proof of T1 (elementary, low risk)

$P-\max\{u_m,P-u_{m-1}\}=\min\{P-u_m,u_{m-1}\}$ holds pointwise in $\mathbb Z$. Summing over $m$ turns P1's bracket into $a_tP-w_{a_t}^{(P)}$. Since $\operatorname{lcm}(g,h^{\#})$ picks up $\max\{u_m,P-u_{m-1}\}$ at $f_m$ and $\deg f_m=d$ is constant along a cycle, $\deg\operatorname{lcm}=\sum_{\mathfrak c}d(\mathfrak c)\big(a(\mathfrak c)P-w_{a(\mathfrak c)}\big)$, while $n=\sum_{\mathfrak c}d(\mathfrak c)a(\mathfrak c)P$. Subtract. $\square$

## 4.2 Proof of T5 / LCD count (elementary, very low risk)

$w_a^{(P)}(u)=0$ iff every term vanishes, i.e. for all $m$: $\min\{u_{m-1},P-u_m\}=0$, i.e. $u_{m-1}=0$ or $u_m=P$. Suppose some $u_i\neq P$. Taking $m=i$ forces $u_{i-1}=0$; then $u_{i-1}\neq P$ (as $P\ge1$), so taking $m=i-1$ forces $u_{i-2}=0$; induct around the cycle, all $u\equiv0$. Otherwise all $u_m=P$, which satisfies the condition. Hence exactly two words. Independence across cycles gives $2^{B}$. $\square$

*Cross-check against an independent result:* this is exactly the quantitative form of **P3 Thm 4.17** ($\,g=g^{\#}$ and $\gcd(g,h)=1$): $g^{\#}=g$ forces the word to be rotation-invariant ($u_0=\dots=u_{a-1}$), and $\gcd(g,h)=1$ forces each $u_m\in\{0,P\}$; together, all-$0$ or all-$P$. **The agreement of two independent derivations is a strong correctness signal.**

## 4.3 Proof of T2 (low risk)

The map $C\mapsto\big(u^{(\mathfrak c)}\big)_{\mathfrak c\in\mathfrak C}$ is a bijection from $\mathscr C_{n,q,\lambda}$ onto $\prod_{\mathfrak c}[0,P]^{a(\mathfrak c)}$ (P1's factorisation is into pairwise coprime monic irreducibles). By T1 the hull dimension is a sum over cycles of a function of the cycle's own word. Independence $\Rightarrow$ the probability generating factorises. $\square$

## 4.4 Proof of T3 (low risk)

Expand $\operatorname{tr}(T^a)=\sum_{u_0,\dots,u_{a-1}}\prod_{m}T[u_{m-1},u_m]$ (cyclic indexing) and note $T[x,y]=z^{\min\{x,P-y\}}$. The rational form of $\mathcal G_P$ and the order-$(P+1)$ recurrence follow from $\operatorname{tr}(tT(I-tT)^{-1})=\sum_{i}\frac{t\lambda_i}{1-t\lambda_i}$ and Cayley–Hamilton. $\deg W_a^{(P)}=\lfloor aP/2\rfloor$ must be proved by exhibiting a maximiser (a word attaining $\lfloor aP/2\rfloor$).

## 4.5 Proof of T4 (low risk)

Direct: $T_1(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\z&1\end{pmatrix}$, characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2-2\lambda+(1-z)$, eigenvalues $1\pm\sqrt z$, trace of $a$-th power as stated; the binomial expansion is the even-part. $\square$

## 4.6 Proof of T7/T8 (medium / high risk)

T7 is calculus on $W$ at $z=1$. T8 is the Lindeberg–Feller triangular-array CLT; the whole difficulty is the arithmetic control of the cycle data $(a(\mathfrak c),\operatorname{ord}_{j(\mathfrak c)}(q))$ as $n$ varies. **Mitigation / fallback ladder (in order):**
1. Prove the CLT under an explicit, checkable hypothesis on the cycle data (accept a conditional theorem).
2. Prove the *variance lower bound* $V\to\infty$ along one explicit infinite family (target: $\lambda=1$, $q$ fixed, $n$ coprime to $q$; then $r=1$, $P=1$, $A=\{j:j\mid n\}$ and $\Delta_1\ge1$ by P3 Thm 3.12 — the machinery of P3 §3.2 is directly reusable here).
3. If 1–2 stall: demote to a **numerical** section (exact distributions + Monte-Carlo + Q–Q evidence), explicitly labelled as such, and keep T1–T7, T9 as the paper's theorems.

## 4.7 Computational-validation strategy (the core de-risking instrument)

**Validation principle:** every enumerator claim is checked against a *structurally independent* brute force that never uses the cycle decomposition: for each code, build $g$, $h$, $h^{\#}$ as **polynomials**, compute $\dim=n-\deg\operatorname{lcm}(g,h^{\#})$, and histogram. Agreement of the two pipelines on every instance is the acceptance criterion for each phase.

This has already been executed in the feasibility probe on 7 instances and passed on all of them (Annex A.3), including 4-cycles and 512-code spaces.

# §5. Exact Parameters, Algorithms and Experiments

## 5.1 Algorithm E1 — cycle data from $(n,q,\lambda,k)$

1. $n'=n/p^{\nu}$ with $\nu=v_p(n)$; $P=p^{\nu}$; $\mu=\lambda^{\,p^{e-\nu}}$ so $\mu^{P}=\lambda$.
2. Factor $x^{n'}-\mu$ over $\mathbb F_q$ (square-free since $\gcd(n',p)=1$): distinct-degree factorisation + equal-degree (Cantor–Zassenhaus for odd $q$; trace-map variant for even $q$).
3. For each monic irreducible $f$, iterate $f\mapsto$ monic$(f^{\#})$ to close the cycle; record $(a,d)$, $d=\deg f=\operatorname{ord}_j(q)$.
4. (Optional, for reporting against P1 notation) recover $D_t,\beta_t(j)$ by grouping cycles of equal $(a,d)$.
**Output:** multiset $\mathfrak C$; $B=|\mathfrak C|$; $\sum a(\mathfrak c)$; predicted $|\mathscr C|=(P+1)^{\sum a(\mathfrak c)}$.

## 5.2 Algorithm E2 — enumerator

$$\texttt{dist}\leftarrow\{0\!:\!1\};\quad \text{for }\mathfrak c\in\mathfrak C:\ \texttt{dist}\leftarrow\texttt{dist}*\big(W_{a(\mathfrak c)}^{(P)}\ \text{with }z\leftarrow y^{d(\mathfrak c)}\big),$$
where $W_a^{(P)}$ is obtained either by (i) brute force over $[0,P]^a$ when $(P+1)^a\le10^{6}$, or (ii) $\operatorname{tr}(T_P(z)^a)$ by binary powering in $\mathbb Z[z]$. Polynomial multiplication in $\mathbb Z[y]$.

## 5.3 Algorithm E3 — independent brute force (validation oracle)

Iterate $u\in\prod_{\mathfrak c}[0,P]^{a(\mathfrak c)}$; build $g=\prod f_m^{u_m}$, $h=\prod f_m^{P-u_m}$; $h^{\#}$ from the polynomial $h$; $\dim=n-\deg\operatorname{lcm}(g,h^{\#})$; histogram. Feasible while $|\mathscr C|\le10^{5}$ (pure Python) or $\le10^{7}$ (NumPy-accelerated).

## 5.4 Algorithm E4 — restricted (P3) sample space

As E2/E3 but with $\tilde T_P=T_P\circ(J-I)$ for $a\ge2$ (equivalently: reject words with $u_m=u_{m+1}$ cyclically).

## 7.3 Discrepancy protocol (v6 rule: never guess, never silently paper over)

Any mismatch between our computation and a printed source value is handled by a fixed protocol:
1. Re-derive by hand; 2. Recompute with the independent oracle E3; 3. Recompute with a second, structurally different method (e.g. direct $\operatorname{lcm}$ vs cycle sum); 4. Only then log it as a **finding** with the full certificate; 5. In the paper it appears as a *Remark*, never as an unannounced change.
Three such candidate discrepancies have already surfaced in probing — they are recorded as **flags**, i.e. as *open questions to be settled in CP3/CP5*, **not** as assertions (Annex A.5).

# §8. Expected Contributions and Limitations

## 8.1 Expected contributions (to be claimed only once proved)

1. **A uniform enumerator** $N_{n,q,\lambda,k}(y)$ for the $k$-Galois hull dimension of $\lambda$-constacyclic codes — solving the enumeration problem P1 leaves open beyond $l=4$, and P2 leaves open over $A$.
2. **A transfer-matrix structure theorem** $W_a^{(P)}=\operatorname{tr}(T_P^a)$ with an explicit rational bivariate generating function and an order-$(P+1)$ recurrence.
3. **A closed form** in the semisimple case: $W_a^{(1)}=(1+\sqrt z)^a+(1-\sqrt z)^a=2\sum_i\binom{a}{2i}z^i$.
4. **Exact closed-form counts** of $k$-Galois LCD ($2^{B}$), self-orthogonal and dual-containing constacyclic codes, over $\mathbb F_q$, over $A$, and over $R_{m,q}$.
5. **Exact moments**; a unified restricted/unrestricted treatment that recovers P3's average formulas as a special case.
6. **A limit law** (conditional CLT) and the $\Theta(n)$ vs $O(1)$ contrast with unstructured linear codes.
7. **A polynomial-time algorithm** replacing exponential enumeration, with a released open-source implementation.
8. **A quantum-code corollary:** the exact number of entanglement-free ($c=0$) EAQECCs obtainable from $\lambda$-constacyclic codes over $A$ and $R_{m,q}$.

## 8.2 Limitations (to be stated openly in the paper)

- **L1 — Counting, not isometry classes.** P2 asks for *non-isometric* counts. We count **codes** (as P1 does over fields). Quotienting by the automorphism group of $A$ / by the Gray-map isometries is **out of scope** and stated as future work.
- **L2 — Standing hypotheses inherited.** $r\mid(1+p^{e-k})$ and $\gcd(n',r)=1$ are required (they come from P1/P2/P3 and guarantee $\operatorname{hull}_k(C)$ is $\lambda$-constacyclic). Codes with $r\nmid(1+p^{e-k})$ are **not** covered; $\dim\operatorname{hull}_k$ there is a genuinely different problem.
- **L3 — No closed form for general $P$.** $W_a^{(P)}$ is given by a transfer matrix and a recurrence, not a product formula. Whether $\det(I-tT_P(z))$ has a closed form for all $P$ is left open (partial data in Annex A.2).
- **L4 — CLT is conditional** unless §4.6 step 2 succeeds; otherwise it is numerical evidence.
- **L5 — Minimum distances / record codes are conditional** on tooling (Magma unavailable). **Mitigation:** the headline application (§8.1.8) needs *no* minimum-distance computation. Any table of $[n,k,d]$ parameters is only reported where $d$ is certified by exhaustive search or by an open-source CAS; otherwise omitted.
- **L6 — Palindromicity / unimodality are NOT claimed**; probe data shows $W_a^{(P)}$ is not palindromic in general.
- **L7 — Novelty boundary (added by audit).** The paper is **not** the first to enumerate codes by hull dimension. Sangwisut–Jitman–Ling–Udomkavanich (*Finite Fields Appl.* 33 (2015) 232–257) enumerate cyclic/negacyclic codes by Euclidean/Hermitian hull dimension using coefficient extraction from a generating function, and their factor $2^{s+t}$ equals our $2^{B}$ in the overlap of the two scopes. Our contributions in that intersection are **extension and unification only**; the uncontested novelties are the transfer-matrix/trace structure, the cycle polynomials and their closed form, $k$-Galois duality with cycle lengths $>2$, the moments, the limit law, and the ring enumerators.
- **L8 — Validation is computational, not CAS-certified at scale.** The released validator is a self-written $\mathbb F_{p^e}$/factorisation stack (Magma is unavailable). It reproduces four published factorisations from P1/P3 exactly, one published enumeration count from PA-1 exactly, and passes $385$ internal factorisation instances; but it is not a substitute for a certified CAS in the final artifact. Anything that will appear as a *table* in the paper should ideally be re-checked in SageMath before submission (OPEN-02).
