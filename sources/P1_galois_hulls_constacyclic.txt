Galois hulls of constacyclic codes over finite fields
Indibar Debnath1 · Om Prakash1   · Habibul Islam1

Abstract
This paper presents a formula for the dimension of Galois hulls of constacyclic codes. For 
this, we have arranged the irreducible factors of xn − λ over the finite field 픽q in a suitable 
way. Also, considering some restrictions on q, the number of constacyclic codes of length 
n over 픽q is calculated for a given Galois hull dimension.
Keywords  Constacyclic codes · Galois inner product · Galois hulls · Cyclotomic 
polynomials · LCD codes
Mathematics Subject Classification  94B05 · 94B15 · 94B60
1  Introduction
The hull of a linear code is the intersection of the code with its dual. Linear complemen-
tary dual (shortly LCD) codes are linear codes with zero hull dimension. These codes have 
many useful applications in cryptography, see [2]. Although, the Euclidean hull of a linear 
code was introduced by Assmus Jr. and Key [1] in 1990. In 1997, Sendrier [21] studied the 
dimension of the hull and showed that the asymptotic proportion of q-ary codes having 
hull dimension l is a positive constant depending only on q and l. Testing the permutation 
equivalence of two linear codes is an interesting problem in coding theory and this prob-
lem was investigated by Leon et al. [11] and also by Petrank and Roth [15]. Hulls of linear 
codes play a key role in determining the complexity of algorithms for checking permuta-
tion equivalence of two linear codes, and we refer [10, 19, 20]. The fact is that most of 
the algorithms do not work if the hull size is large. Recently, Guenda et al. [6] applied the 
hulls of linear codes in constructing good entanglement-assisted quantum error-correcting 
codes. Due to this wide range of applications, the hulls of linear codes and their properties 
have been extensively studied.

On the other hand, constacyclic codes are an important class of linear codes which have 
various applications in information technology. Moreover, this family of codes contains 
cyclic codes with rich algebraic structures and well-known applications, see [7, 16]. The 
study on hulls of cyclic and constacyclic codes has recently attracted many researchers. In 
2015, the hull dimensions of cyclic and negacyclic codes of length n over 픽q were deter-
mined for both the Euclidean and Hermitian inner products by Sangwisut et al. [18]. They 
have also calculated the number of cyclic codes of length n over 픽q having hulls of a given 
dimension. Later, the average hull dimension of cyclic codes by using Euclidean inner prod-
ucts was studied by Skersys in [22]. Jitman and Sangwisut [9] derived the average dimen-
sion of the Hermitian hull of cyclic codes over finite fields of square order. Consequently, 
in 2018, they calculated the average dimension of the Hermitian hull of constacyclic codes 
over finite fields of square order in [8].
The notion of k-Galois inner product, which is a generalization of both the Euclidean 
and Hermitian inner products, was introduced by Fan and Zhang [5] in 2017. In 2018, 
Liu and Pan [14] studied the Galois hulls of linear codes over finite fields. Recently, in 
2020, Ding and Lu [4] studied the Galois hulls of cyclic codes over finite fields. However, 
the investigation of Galois hulls of constacyclic codes is not available in the literature yet. 
Hence, this motivates us to study the Galois hulls of constacyclic codes over finite fields.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we have recalled a few basic definitions 
and some well-known results. Section 3 contains the rearrangement of irreducible factors 
of xn − λ. We use this particular form of factorization to propose a formula for the dimen-
sions of the k-Galois hulls of a specific class of λ-constacyclic codes of length n over finite 
fields. Further, we have calculated the number of λ-constacyclic codes of a given Galois 
hull dimension over certain finite fields. Section 4 concludes our work.

4  Conclusion
We have studied the Galois hulls of constacyclic codes over finite fields. More specifically, 
we have proposed a formula for the dimensions of the Galois hulls of a certain class of 
constacyclic codes. Further, we have also calculated the number of constacyclic codes of 
a given Galois hull dimension under certain restrictions on alphabet size. Therefore, as an 
open problem, the dimensions of the Galois hulls of all families of constacyclic codes are 
yet to be formulated. One can also look into the problem of enumeration of constacyclic 
codes of any given Galois hull dimension over all finite fields.
